Five theories. Five different ways into a human head.

Every one of these is a real story from this week, with the real facts underneath it and the theory marked as a theory. What differs is the emotion each one is built to trigger — because that, not the subject, is what decides whether a thing gets passed on.

  • AWE + STAKE — $70 billion. One owner. Nobody knows who.
  • AWE — Six thousand planets. Not one moon.
  • IDENTITY — Wrong by nearly two feet.
  • UNEASE — Hundreds of messages. One subject line. Nobody knows.
  • ANXIETY — Fifteen hundred ships. Sitting still. Growing something.
  • PERSONAL THREAT — The spray is not failing. It is teaching.

Method

What actually makes a thing get shared

Berger & Milkman (2012) found high-arousal emotion drives sharing — awe, anger and anxiety all increase it, while sadness decreases it. So every script is built on one specific high-arousal emotion, chosen deliberately, and no two of these five use the same one.

Information-gap curiosity

Loewenstein: curiosity is an unpleasant state that fires when you become aware of a *specific* gap in what you know. Vague mystery doesn't itch. A missing number does.

Applied: Name the gap precisely and early — 'six thousand planets and not one moon', 'wrong by nearly two feet'.

Proportionality bias

People expect big effects to have big causes. When the official explanation is small relative to the event, it feels like a cover even when it's true. This is the actual engine of conspiracy belief.

Applied: Point at a real size mismatch. Never manufacture one — the mismatch has to survive a fact-check or the whole thing collapses.

Agency detection

Humans over-attribute events to an intending actor. 'Someone chose this' sticks harder than 'this happened'.

Applied: Give the sentence a subject wherever it's honest to. 'We built the categories.' 'We're the ones running it.'

Zeigarnik / open loop

Unfinished things are remembered better and returned to. A resolved video is a finished thought and gets closed.

Applied: Never answer the question. The last line reopens it. The comments become the resolution, and that's the engagement.

Social currency

Sharing signals status — it says you know something the reader doesn't. The payoff has to be repeatable in one sentence or it doesn't get passed on.

Applied: Every script ends on a line you could say out loud to someone at work tomorrow.

Guardrails: No named living person is the subject of any theory here, nothing turns on a fresh tragedy, and every factual claim is checkable against the source listed on each card. The theory beat is always marked as a reading, not a finding.

Teardown

Every story gets pulled apart before a word is written

Seven passes over the same facts, each asking a different question. The point is that one teardown yields several videos, not one — and that pass five is the whole product.

PassThe questionWhat it yields
1. TimelineWhat happened, in what order, on what date?The spine. Gaps in the timeline are the best angles you will find.
2. EntitiesWho and what is named? Who is conspicuously *not* named?Absences are angles. A story with no named decision-maker is a story with a hole.
3. MoneyWho paid, who gained, how much, and when relative to the event?Numbers are the most shareable unit of a conspiracy. Timing beats amount.
4. The official accountWhat is the stated explanation, in its own words?You must be able to state it fairly. If you strawman it, the video dies on contact with a commenter.
5. What it does not explainWhich specific fact does the official account leave standing?**This is the video.** Not "they're lying" — "here is the thing their answer does not cover."
6. BeneficiaryIf this were deliberate, who is better off?Turns a passive event into an agent-driven one, which is what the brain latches onto.
7. The open loopWhat single question can honestly be left unanswered?The closing line. Never resolve it.

The rule that keeps it publishable: pass five has to be a real gap. If the official account covers everything, there is no video and we pick another story. And we interrogate decisions, timing, money and silence — never allege a specific crime by a named living person, because calling it a theory does not change how that reads.

00AWE + STAKE

$70 billion. One owner. Nobody knows who.

Satoshi Nakamoto's ~1.1M BTC, untouched since 2009

The gap it opens

Sixteen years, thousands of separate addresses, several bull runs — and not one coin has moved.

Why this one should travel

Incomprehensible money plus an unknown person, attached to an asset millions already own. Crypto audiences share this reflexively because it is about their own bag.

Script as recorded

One person owns more Bitcoin than almost anyone alive. In sixteen years, they have barely touched it.

On the third of January 2009, someone mined the very first Bitcoin block. Inside it they left a line from that morning's newspaper. Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks.

Then they kept mining. Mostly alone, for well over a year.

In 2013 a cryptographer went back through the earliest blocks and found a fingerprint. One machine. One pattern. Starting at block zero, and going cold around May 2010.

That single miner ended up holding somewhere around one point one million Bitcoin. At today's price, about seventy billion dollars.

Out of all of it, less than a thousand coins have ever moved. Less than one tenth of one percent.

The official reading is the ordinary one. Whoever it was walked away, and either lost the keys, or chose never to touch them.

There is another reading. That the stillness is the product. The moment that wallet really moves, the world learns Satoshi is alive, and everything reprices in about four seconds.

Nobody has ever been confirmed as Satoshi. The coins are still sitting there. You can go and look at them tonight.

The verified facts underneath it
  • Genesis block mined 3 January 2009, embedding 'The Times 03/Jan/2009 Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks' — verified byte-for-byte against the live chain
  • April 2013: cryptographer Sergio Demian Lerner found a single-miner fingerprint across the earliest blocks (first 36,288, extended to 50,000 a week later)
  • The fingerprint stops being DETECTABLE around block 54,316 / 3 May 2010 — that block figure is Whale Alert's, not Lerner's, who only said 'approximately May 2010'
  • ~1.1 million BTC is an ESTIMATE, not a firm figure; credible sources span roughly 600,000 to 1,125,150
  • At ~$63,900/BTC (six independent price sources, 29 Jul 2026) that is ~$70.3 billion
  • Under ~950 of those coins have ever moved — so 'never moved' is wrong; 'almost never' is right
  • A 2021 PLoS ONE paper (Óskarsdóttir & Mallett) finds the anomaly recurs after 2010, so 'the pattern stopped' is a reading, not a settled fact
  • No living person has ever been confirmed to be Satoshi Nakamoto

Source: Lerner's 2013 Patoshi analysis; Whale Alert; Óskarsdóttir & Mallett, PLoS ONE 2021; live chain data; six independent BTC price sources — all independently re-verified

01AWE

Six thousand planets. Not one moon.

First plausible exomoon detection, published in Nature, July 2026

The gap it opens

We found six thousand planets and zero moons. That ratio is the itch.

Why this one should travel

The strongest positive sharing emotion in Berger & Milkman's data. Scale does the work — 71 light years, Jupiter-mass, six thousand to one.

Script as recorded

We have found more than six thousand planets around other stars.

In all that time, orbiting all of them, we had never confidently found a single moon.

That changed this month. Seventy-one light years away, astronomers watched a brown dwarf — a star that failed to ignite — and caught something circling it. They found it by the wobble. The tug of something heavy going round.

Here is the part they had to argue about. It is roughly the mass of Jupiter.

So the paper does not call it a moon. It calls it an exosatellite. Officially, because there is no agreed definition for a Jupiter-sized body orbiting a failed star.

There is another reading. That we did not spend thirty years failing to find moons because moons are small and dim. But because we were looking for the wrong thing, at the wrong size, the whole time.

Six thousand planets. One moon. And we do not have a word for it.

The verified facts underneath it
  • More than 6,000 exoplanets have been confirmed; no exomoon had ever been confidently detected
  • The system is CD-35 2722, about 71 light years away
  • The host is a brown dwarf — an object too small to ignite as a star
  • Found by radial velocity: the wobble of the host as something heavy orbits it, using VLT/CRIRES+ spectra
  • Minimum mass about 0.9 Jupiter masses; orbital period around 170 days
  • The Nature paper calls it an 'exosatellite', not an exomoon, because no agreed definition covers a Jupiter-mass body orbiting a brown dwarf

Source: Nature — 'Planetary-mass exosatellite detected around the substellar companion of a star'; ESO release; Reuters, Time, Nature news coverage

02IDENTITY

Wrong by nearly two feet.

1.43-million-year-old Paranthropus boisei footprints, PNAS, July 2026

The gap it opens

A species studied for sixty years turns out to be two feet taller than the textbook. What else is a guess with a picture next to it?

Why this one should travel

Not fear — self-concept. Stories about what humans are get shared because agreeing with them says something about the sharer.

Script as recorded

For sixty years we have drawn this creature the same way. Squat. Four foot two. About a hundred pounds. A jaw built for cracking nuts.

Then someone found the footprints.

Twenty-one of them, pressed into mud in northern Kenya one and a half million years ago, and preserved. Eight individuals. Walking together.

Measure a footprint and you get a body. The bodies come out at six feet. A hundred and sixty-five pounds.

We were not slightly wrong. We were wrong by nearly two feet.

The official reading is straightforward. Skeletons are rare, footprints are better evidence, and an old estimate has been corrected.

The harder reading is what came with it. All eight of them were male, moving as a group, and we had no idea. If we can be wrong by two feet about a species we have studied for six decades, the honest question is what else in that textbook is a guess with a picture next to it.

The verified facts underneath it
  • 21 fossil footprints from 8 individuals, found in the Koobi Fora Formation, northern Kenya
  • Dated to about 1.43 million years ago
  • Published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences
  • New estimate: up to 6 feet (1.8 m) tall and as much as 165 lb (75 kg)
  • Previous estimate: males around 4.2 feet (131 cm) and about 108 lb (49 kg)
  • The eight individuals crossed the site at the same time, travelling as a group, and appear to have been all male

Source: PNAS study; CNN, Smithsonian Magazine, New Scientist, Popular Science, Phys.org coverage

03UNEASE

Hundreds of messages. One subject line. Nobody knows.

The 1996 'Markovian Parallax Denigrate' Usenet flood, back in circulation July 2026

The gap it opens

Hundreds of coordinated messages on a network where almost everyone was identifiable, and no one was ever identified.

Why this one should travel

Low-grade dread, not fear. It works because the artefact is still there and the viewer can go and touch it — which converts unease into an action.

Script as recorded

In August 1996, hundreds of messages appeared across Usenet at once. Every single one carried the same subject line. Markovian Parallax Denigrate.

Open one and you get this. Jitterbugging. McKinley. Abe. Break. Newtonian. Inferring. Caw. Update.

Real English words, in an order that means nothing. Hundreds of them. Arriving together. On a network where almost everybody was identifiable.

The likely explanation is the dull one. Markov chains were a well known toy at the time. You feed a program some text and it emits words that look like language and are not. Somebody was probably testing one. Reporters who went back years later concluded the mystery had been inflated by its own retelling.

Here is the other reading. If you wanted to move something past people who were watching, this is exactly what it would look like. Noise, at volume, with a label on it.

Nobody has ever established who sent them. The archive is still there. You can go and read it tonight.

The verified facts underneath it
  • August 1996: hundreds of messages appeared across Usenet newsgroups
  • Every one carried the identical subject line 'Markovian Parallax Denigrate'
  • The bodies were strings of unrelated English words — 'jitterbugging McKinley Abe break Newtonian inferring caw update'
  • Contemporary users mostly read it as spam or a Markov-chain text experiment
  • Posts were traced to a university email account, and a later A.V. Club investigation argued the mystery has been inflated by its own retelling
  • No one has ever established who sent them or why. The archive is still publicly readable

Source: Wikipedia 'Markovian Parallax Denigrate'; Atlas Obscura; Boing Boing; A.V. Club investigation

04ANXIETY

Fifteen hundred ships. Sitting still. Growing something.

Biological Invasions paper on a Strait of Hormuz bioinvasion super-spreader event, July 2026

The gap it opens

You have read about this strait for five months and never once about the thing growing on the hulls.

Why this one should travel

Delayed, invisible and already in motion. Anxiety outperforms anger for sharing when the threat is impersonal and the viewer can't act on it.

Script as recorded

You have been hearing about the Strait of Hormuz for five months. Oil price. Shipping lanes. Military posture.

Here is the part that is not in those reports.

Around fifteen hundred ships have been sitting still in warm water since February. A moving hull stays relatively clean. A stationary one does not. Barnacles, algae, seaweed, bacteria. They colonise it.

Twenty-four marine scientists have now published a warning in the journal Biological Invasions. When the strait reopens, those fifteen hundred hulls do not just carry cargo. They carry everything that grew on them, to every port they visit.

They call it a super-spreader event.

The official framing is a shipping problem with an environmental footnote.

The other reading is that we have spent five months building the largest accidental species transport system in history, in full view of everyone, and the first anybody will notice is when something arrives somewhere it has never been.

It is still sitting there. Growing.

The verified facts underneath it
  • The Strait of Hormuz has been closed since 28 February 2026
  • Roughly 1,500 ships are stranded in the Persian/Arabian Gulf, with hundreds more anchored in the Gulf of Oman
  • 24 marine scientists, led by the University of Maryland Center for Environmental Science, published the warning
  • Paper: 'Closure of the Strait of Hormuz may trigger a bioinvasion super-spreader event', journal Biological Invasions
  • Stationary hulls in warm water accumulate biofouling — bacteria, algae, seaweed, barnacles — far faster than moving ones
  • When the strait reopens those hulls carry that growth to every port they visit

Source: Biological Invasions (Springer); University of Maryland Center for Environmental Science release; WHOI; Guardian, ABC News, Popular Science coverage

05PERSONAL THREAT

The spray is not failing. It is teaching.

Research on rapid enzyme-driven insecticide resistance in mosquitoes, July 2026

The gap it opens

Everyone assumes a spray that stops working has worn off. The reverse is true, and the mechanism is the unsettling part.

Why this one should travel

Proximity is the lever. The other four are far away in space, time or scale — this one is in the viewer's own garden this weekend.

Script as recorded

The spray in your garden is not failing. It is teaching.

Researchers looked at what happens inside a mosquito when it meets a common household insecticide. The insect does not simply survive it. It switches something on.

The survivors start producing more of the enzymes that break the chemical down into something harmless, and then pass that on.

It is not a mutation at the target. It is the detox system, turned up.

The official advice is the sensible advice. Rotate the chemicals. Remove standing water. Do not rely on one product.

There is a harder reading. Every fogging is a selection event. The ones that die are the ones that could not cope. The ones left are the ones that could.

And they are the ones breeding tonight.

The verified facts underneath it
  • Researchers examined what happens inside mosquitoes exposed to a common household pyrethroid insecticide
  • Survivors rapidly up-regulate detoxification enzymes — cytochrome P450s and esterases among them
  • Resistance here comes from over-expressing those enzymes rather than from a mutation at the insecticide's target site
  • The enzymes break the compound down into harmless products the insect then excretes
  • Standard guidance is to rotate insecticide classes, remove standing water and not rely on a single product
  • Every application selects for the individuals that survived it

Source: Frontiers (2026) work on detoxification enzymes in Aedes aegypti exposed to alpha-cypermethrin; Phys.org, The Scientist, The Debrief coverage
